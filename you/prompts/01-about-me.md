# Prompt 1: Build your about-me

This is the first thing to run in a fresh project. It interviews you and writes an
`about-me.md` file, the standing context Claude reads on every run. Think of it as
the Projects "custom instructions" box, except it is now a file you own, in your
folder, that Claude reads *and updates*.

Paste this into your project and answer the questions:

```text
I'm setting up this project as my working space, and I want you to have standing
context about me on every run. Your job right now is to build that.

Interview me to create an "about-me.md" file in this folder. Ask your questions in
small batches (3-4 at a time, not all at once), and keep going until you have
enough to write something genuinely useful, not generic. Cover:

- Who I am: name, role, what I actually do day to day
- The businesses / projects I work across, and what each one is for
- How I work: how I like things done, what good output looks like to me, what I can't stand
- My voice: how I write and talk, so you can draft things that sound like me (ask me to paste a sample if useful)
- The people in my orbit who come up a lot (colleagues, clients, partners) and who they are to me
- What I mainly want your help with

Rules:
- Ask, don't assume. If you can infer something, confirm it rather than guessing.
- Keep it tight and factual. This is a reference file you'll read every session, not an essay.
- When you have enough, show me the draft before you save it. On my OK, write it to about-me.md in this folder.
- Tell me at the end how to keep adding to it over time.
```

## Why the file beats the old custom-instructions box

In Projects, that context was locked in a settings box you had to copy between
projects. Here it is a file. When something changes, you just say "add this to my
about-me" and Claude edits the file. It travels with the folder, and it grows.

## The moment it clicks

The first time Claude drafts something in your voice without being told the
background, that is the "oh, it just *knows* me now" feeling. That is the whole
point of context-as-files: you stop starting cold every conversation.
