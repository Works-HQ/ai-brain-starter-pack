# From you to your company

A personal AI system becomes a company brain when the useful context stops being only about you. The structure begins to hold shared knowledge: what the company does, how decisions get made, who owns what, what is true now and how the work should run.

You do not need to make that jump on day one. Start personally. Learn which files you actually use, which updates matter and which habits keep the context current. Move to the company level when other people or repeated work need the same memory.

## The signs you have outgrown the personal layer

The personal setup is enough while one person can hold the missing context. It starts to strain when:

- several people need the same background before they can act
- decisions live across meeting notes, messages and individual memories
- new work keeps requiring the same company briefing
- tasks depend on ownership, handoffs or waiting items
- your root context file becomes a crowded map of the whole operation

These are routing problems, not prompt problems. Adding a longer instruction will not fix knowledge that has no shared home.

## Mine the company you already have

Do not answer the problem by opening a blank template and trying to write the company from memory. The company already exists in documents. Plans, proposals, policies, meeting notes, role descriptions, customer research and old decisions contain the raw material.

Put copies of those sources in one place. Ask the AI to read them, identify durable facts, flag contradictions and draft a clean company brief. Check every claim against the source. Then keep the useful result in a small set of scoped files.

This is mining, not importing. A forty-page strategy document may contribute five facts that still matter. An old proposal may reveal language worth keeping and an offer that no longer exists. The AI can do the first pass quickly. You decide what is true now.

Blank templates ask you to invent a complete system before you have used it. Mining gives you a grounded first version and shows where the real gaps are.

## Keep the same shape, add shared routes

The company layer does not replace the personal one. It extends the same pattern.

Your personal files still hold your preferences and working context. The company brain holds shared facts and scoped knowledge. The operating system separates current context, decisions, open tasks, waiting items and history. The root context file routes the AI to the right place instead of trying to contain everything.

The maintenance loop also becomes shared. A meeting debrief can update the decision record and task list. A weekly review can catch stale context. A reflection can record a workflow improvement for the next person who runs it.

## Cross the bridge when the work asks for it

Build the company brain around real pressure. Start with one company file mined from real documents. Add one person file when ownership or working preferences matter. Add the operating files when decisions and handoffs need a shared record. Install skills only after the team has run the underlying workflow manually.

The personal layer teaches the habit: keep good files and update them through the work. The company layer gives that habit a shared home. Once the knowledge no longer depends on one person's memory, the AI and the people around it can start from the same truth.
