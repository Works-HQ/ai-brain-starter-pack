The three homes

Every piece of information has one home. That is the rule that stops it rotting.

01

Current state

CLAUDE.md

Status, last contact, next action. The truth as of right now, kept in a single living block.

Overwritten in place

02

History

debrief-history.log

The dated story of what actually happened, session by session. Nothing here is ever edited.

Append-only

03

Open tasks

TASKS.md

What is still on your plate, grouped by area. A task that is done leaves the file.

Open items only

Reference (what things are) and live state (what is happening now) never mix. Slow context is what changes rarely, your clients, your people, your pricing. Fast context is this week's status and today's tasks. Keep the two speeds apart and the system stays legible instead of turning into a junk drawer.

The loop

Two skills, run every session, and the system compounds.

Structure alone is dead weight. The loop is what turns a folder of files into a brain.

Step 1reflect

Captures how you want to work. The corrections and preferences you fixed mid-session land in your standing context, with your sign-off. Correct once, never again.

→

Step 2debrief

Files what happened into the three homes. State overwrites, history appends, tasks stay open. The discipline is enforced for you, so nothing rots.

Run reflect first, while the corrections are fresh, then debrief. Do that every session and the pair becomes a loop that pays you back.

Quickstart

Five steps, about twenty minutes.

  1. Point Cowork at a folder

    That folder is the workspace. While you are learning, use a folder of copies, not your only copy of anything.

  2. Build your about-me

    Run the setup prompt to create about-me.md, your standing context Claude reads at the start of every session.

  3. Set up the structure

    Create the three homes, a company or area CLAUDE.md, TASKS.md, and the history log.

  4. Install the two skills

    Drop in debrief and reflect. In Cowork you trigger them with plain language.

  5. Do real work, then reflect and debrief

    Run reflect first to catch the corrections, then debrief to file what happened. Repeat, and it compounds.
